# Comparing options

> Loaded when: Comparing options, or judging a claim that one option is better.

From Luu's essays before 2026; none comes from his agent work. Most of it is ordinary decision analysis, kept where his posts show someone getting it wrong, and worded no wider than verification allowed. Each option's evidence is a claim: run [the checks](../SKILL.md#the-six-checks) on it first.

The moves have no order.

- **Refuse "better in all ways".** Split the claim into the parts of the trade-off; "the real question is how to weight the various parts of the trade-off, not which side is better in all ways". In a ranking that rests on your weights, state them and show the per-item detail so others can re-rank. His failure case: the claim that startups beat big companies on every axis.
- **Look for an option that dominates, or a design that eases the conflict.** Working at a big company and investing in startups gets the startup upside without the trade. A third design may only reduce the pain: "These solutions aren't ideal".
- **Find the cost term that dominates at your scale, and say where it flips.** For his company, engineering cost "completely dominates" the cost of the systems; serving cost becomes a concern only at about three orders of magnitude more growth (illustration of a flip point, one company).
- **When the answer depends on context, name the variable and give the winner on each side of it.** LRU beats 2-random eviction on smaller caches, 2-random wins on larger ones. "It depends on what kind of work you want to do", against advice that big companies have boring work.
- **For an option that promises less effort, count the effort of verifying and maintaining it.** Intrinsics: "you have to manually check the result on every platform and every compiler", which is more work than writing the assembly. A buggy bought tool can still be "the right choice at the time".
- **"Easy", "I could build that in a weekend": list the sub-problems and the work off the happy path, at real scale.** "Features are often much more complex than outsiders realize"; people "generally don't know what sub-problems need to be solved". The *sounds-easy* post is self-labelled stream-of-consciousness; *cocktail-ideas* carries the same move at normal standard.

**Where values conflict and cannot be priced, nothing here resolves it.** Settle the facts, state your weight, show the detail, and say that the comparison ends there.
